1.1 The Century-Old Stiffness Catastrophe

In 1895 and 1901, Carl Runge and Wilhelm Kutta formulated the classical explicit Runge-Kutta family of numerical integrators. For an initial value problem (IVP) governing dynamical state $\mathbf{y}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$: $$\frac{d\mathbf{y}}{dt} = \mathbf{f}(t, \mathbf{y}), \quad \mathbf{y}(t_0) = \mathbf{y}0$$ the standard 4th-order Butcher algorithm evaluates four stage velocity tangents: $$\mathbf{k}_1 = h \mathbf{f}(t_n, \mathbf{y}_n)$$ $$\mathbf{k}_2 = h \mathbf{f}\left(t_n + \frac{h}{2}, \mathbf{y}_n + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{k}_1\right)$$ $$\mathbf{k}_3 = h \mathbf{f}\left(t_n + \frac{h}{2}, \mathbf{y}_n + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{k}_2\right)$$ $$\mathbf{k}_4 = h \mathbf{f}(t_n + h, \mathbf{y}_n + \mathbf{k}_3)$$ $$\mathbf{y}$$} = \mathbf{y}_n + \frac{\mathbf{k}_1 + 2\mathbf{k}_2 + 2\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4}{6

For non-dimensional step parameter $z = \lambda h$, the amplification factor is given by the truncated Taylor polynomial: $$R(z) = 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{6} + \frac{z^4}{24}$$

To bypass this barrier, traditional computational engineering relies on implicit integrators, such as Backward Differentiation Formulas (BDF) or Gauss-Radau collocation.
